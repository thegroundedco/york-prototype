About York Barbell. New Era. Same Standard.

For more than ninety years, York Barbell has been making the iron that built American strength. Founded in 1932 in York, Pennsylvania, by Bob Hoffman — the man the International Weightlifting Federation called the "Father of World Weightlifting" — we've spent nearly a century outfitting Olympic teams, commercial gyms, garages, basements, and every kind of lifter in between.

The gear has gotten better over the years. The standard hasn't moved. We still make bars, plates, racks, and benches in the same town we started in, to the same spec we've always held: if it doesn't belong in a serious gym, it doesn't leave the building. This is the iron, made by the people who've been making it the longest.

Our Mission

Our mission is simple: make the best strength equipment in the world, and make it accessible to anyone serious about training.

Bob Hoffman believed that strength wasn't a privilege reserved for athletes — it was a tool every person could use to live longer, move better, and feel more capable in their own body. Nine decades later, we still build to that belief. Every bar, every plate, every bench is engineered for the person on the other end of it: the new lifter learning their first squat, the parent maintaining what they've built, the lifelong athlete training to keep doing the things they love.

We don't chase trends. We make iron that lasts — for the body, for the gym, for the long arc of a life spent in motion.

History

Ninety years of strength, made in one town in Pennsylvania.

1932: Where It All Began

Bob Hoffman starts making barbells in a Pennsylvania oil burner factory and shipping them to anyone who wanted to get stronger. Within a few years, the small operation in New York becomes the Unofficial Home of American Weightlifting — A place lifters travel to from across the country just to train under the same roof where the gear is being built.

1936-1968: The Olympic Years

From the 1936 Berlin games through the 1960s, Bob Hoffman coaches the U.S. Olympic Weightlifting team and York becomes the most decorated weightlifting club in American history. More than forty national champions and a long line of olympic medalists train on York equipment, lift under the York name, and out American strength on the world map.

Today: A New Era

Nine decades later, we're still in York. Still making the bar, the plates, the bench. The world looks different — Most of our gear now lives in basements, garages, and spare rooms instead of competition platforms — But the work is the same. Build the best strength equipment in the world. Put it in the hands of people who want to use it.

York Barbell is a manufacturing company, a community, and a ninety-year-old promise. We're engineers and machinists and lifters, working out of the same Pennsylvania town where Bob Hoffman started the company in 1932. Most of us train. All of us care about the people who do. Every piece of equipment that leaves our facility passes through hands that take this work personally — because the people building it are the same kind of people buying it.

What that means for you: when you order a bar from York, you're not buying from a distribution warehouse. You're buying from a factory floor. From a company that's been doing this for nine decades and still makes equipment that ends up in the hands of Olympic athletes, commercial gyms, and the lifter who just wants the real thing in their home gym.

Bob Hoffman didn't just start a barbell company. He built a movement.

An Olympic-caliber athlete himself, Hoffman founded York Barbell in 1932 and spent the next fifty years turning a small Pennsylvania town into the capital of American weightlifting. He coached the U.S. Olympic Weightlifting Team from 1936 through the 1960s. He founded Strength & Health magazine, which became the leading fitness publication of its era. He served on the President's Council on Physical Fitness and Sports under multiple administrations. The International Weightlifting Federation gave him a title that stuck: the Father of World Weightlifting.

But what Hoffman cared about most wasn't medals or magazines. It was the everyday person — the postal worker, the welder, the schoolteacher — discovering for the first time what their own body was capable of with a barbell in their hands. That belief is still the soul of this company. The gear has evolved. The standard hasn't. The point of all of it remains the same: get strong, stay strong, live well.

Bob Hoffman passed away in 1985. His statue still stands at the entrance of our headquarters in York, Pennsylvania. The work continues.
