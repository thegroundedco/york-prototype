Established in 1932.
Reimagined for today.

Long before home gyms had a category, Bob Hoffman was making barbells in a Pennsylvania oil burner factory and shipping them to anyone who wanted to get stronger. He coached the U.S. Olympic weightlifting team, wrote the books, ran the magazine, and built the company that turned a small city in Pennsylvania into the place people came to learn how to lift.

Day one starts here.

The room you train in today becomes the body you live in tomorrow.

A real home gym needs the right pieces working together. Most beginners build out in three layers: strength, conditioning, and mobility. Cover those, and you can train almost any goal from one room.

Step 1

Strength Training.

Strength is the foundation everything else sits on. It builds bone density, protects your joints, and makes every other kind of training easier. You don't need a full rack on day one — a quality adjustable dumbbell, a sturdy bench, and a few starter accessories will take you further than most people get in their first year of a commercial gym membership.

Step 2

Cardiovascular Conditioning.

Cardio doesn't have to mean a treadmill humming in the corner. The most effective conditioning gear is also the most compact: short, hard intervals beat long, distracted ones every time. Pick one tool you'll actually use, learn how to push it, and you'll build a stronger engine than most gym-goers manage on machines they pretend to love but actually hate.

Step 3

Mobility & Stability.

This is the layer most beginners skip, and the one that decides whether you're still training in ten years or sidelined by a tweaked back. Mobility work keeps your joints moving the way they're supposed to. Stability work keeps the small muscles strong enough to protect the big ones. Five minutes a day, real returns.
